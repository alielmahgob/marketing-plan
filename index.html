Product Vision Board

VISION
“A world where growing older doesn’t mean growing apart.”

Target Group

  • Primary: Seniors 65+ living independently in Europe
  • Paying customer: Adult children (35–55)
  • Secondary: Grandchildren, neighbors, trusted clinicians
  • Launch market: Nordics + DACH region

Needs

  • Seniors: independence without isolation
  • Adult children: peace of mind without over-monitoring
  • Grandchildren: emotional connection, not surveillance
  • Clinicians: context between appointments
  • Unique angle: balancing dignity with safety

Product

  • Two-sided app: Senior mode + Family mode
  • Core: check-in, SOS, vitals, services, video calling
  • Fully static demo built in Flutter
  • Unique: designed for the senior first, family second

Business Goals

  • Free for seniors
  • Subscription: €9.99/mo Family Plan
  • Commission: ~15% on each booked service
  • Strategic goal: default European care app by 2030

Astrid Hansen
74 · Bergen, Norway The Independent Senior
“A widow who refuses to give up her independence but wants her family to stop worrying, without calling her three times a day.”
Background
Astrid lives alone in the home she’s had for 40 years. Sharper than her son gives her credit for. Walks to the bakery every morning. Values her routines fiercely. Not afraid of technology, afraid of being treated like she can’t handle it.
User Story
“As Astrid, I want to let my family know I’m okay with a single tap each morning, so they stop worrying and I keep my independence.”
Goals
Stay in her home, independence
Reassure her family without effort
Get help fast when she needs it
Frustrations
Being infantilized by apps and relatives
Family calling constantly
Asking for help feels like losing
Magnus Hansen
47 · Oslo, Norway The Worried Son
“He just wants to glance at his phone and know mom is okay.”
Background
Project manager who moved to Oslo 15 years ago. Calls his mother on Sundays and set up Wasl for the family. His phone is the one that buzzes if something goes wrong.
User Story
“As Magnus, I want a single dashboard showing my mother’s daily status and vitals, so I can feel close to her without calling every day.”
Goals
Peace of mind in 10 seconds
Catch problems early, before they become emergencies
Coordinate with family without many messages
Frustrations
Distance guilt, he can’t be there, so he over-monitors
False alarms, doesn’t want to drive hours for nothing
Information scattered across calls and messages
Ingrid Hansen
45 · Oslo, Norway The Hands-On Family Member
“The daughter-in-law who actually makes things happen, books the grocery delivery and quietly handles what Magnus worries about.”
Background
Closer to Astrid than most daughters‑in‑law. Where Magnus monitors, Ingrid acts, she notices needs and arranges help.
User Story
“As Ingrid, I want to book services for Astrid from my phone, so I can help without making her feel dependent.”
Goals
Act on Astrid’s behalf without fuss
Take load off Magnus, he watches, she does
Coordinate care so the family stays in sync
Frustrations
Doing invisible work nobody sees
Feeling like a secondary user in an app made for “the son”
Over-permissioned tools, she wants to book a cleaner, not read vitals
Sofia Hansen
19 · Berlin, Germany (studying abroad) The Distant Grandchild
“The granddaughter who doesn’t want a monitoring app, she wants a reason to call her grandmother more often.”
Background
Design student in Berlin who misses her grandmother. She prefers short, meaningful updates (e.g., “Took a morning walk”) over activity dashboards.
User Story
“As Sofia, I want daily highlights and a one-tap call so I can stay close while abroad.”
Goals
Stay emotionally close, not surveil
See human activities and highlights
Frustrations
Monitoring apps feel invasive
Family chats are chaotic
Lars Berg
68 · Bergen, Norway The Trusted Neighbor
“Astrid’s neighbor of thirty years, the person with a spare key to her front door and a place on her emergency call chain when family can’t answer.”
Background
Retired ship mechanic. Lives two houses down from Astrid. They’ve known each other since before Magnus was born. When Astrid added Lars to her Wasl emergency contacts, he took it seriously, he checks his phone more carefully now, keeps it louder at night, and knows exactly what to do if an alert comes through.
User Story
“As Lars, I want to be contacted only when Astrid truly needs help and her family can’t reach her, so I can be ready to act without intruding on her daily life.”
Goals
Be genuinely useful in an emergency, not just a name on a list
Know clearly what’s expected of him, no ambiguous alerts
Respect Astrid’s privacy, only emergencies, not her daily life
Frustrations
Group chats with the family feel intrusive when everything is fine
Wants a simple, minimal role, not another app to monitor
Worried about false alarms, or missing a real one

Fifteen Stories. Five Lives.

A1
As Astrid, I want to tell my family I’m okay with a single tap each morning, so they stop worrying and I keep my independence.
A2
As Astrid, I want one clearly visible button to call for help in an emergency, so I can reach my family fast without dialing numbers.
A3
As Astrid, I want to request a grocery delivery from inside the app, so I don’t have to call or travel when I’m tired.
M1
As Magnus, I want one screen showing my mother’s current status, so I can be reassured in ten seconds.
M2
As Magnus, I want to be notified if my mother hasn’t checked in by noon, so I know when to actually worry.
M3
As Magnus, I want to see my mother’s activity trends over weeks, so I can spot slow changes before they become problems.
I1
As Ingrid, I want to book services for Astrid from my phone, so I can help without making her feel dependent.
I2
As Ingrid, I want to see which family member booked or handled each task, so we coordinate without duplicating effort.
I3
As Ingrid, I want to add personal notes to a service request, so the provider serves Astrid the way we would.
S1
As Sofia, I want to see small human updates about my grandmother’s day, so I have a reason to call her instead of a duty to.
S2
As Sofia, I want to start a video call directly from an activity entry, so I can reach her while the moment is fresh.
S3
As Sofia, I want to send a heart or short voice note to a timeline entry, so I can show affection without a full call.
L1
As Lars, I want a clear emergency alert with Astrid’s location if her family can’t be reached, so I can check on her quickly.
L2
As Lars, I want a one-tap way to tell the family I’m on my way, so they know the situation is handled.
L3
As Lars, I want a friendly nudge to check in on Astrid if something feels off, so I can drop by informally.

Why This Sprint Works

“A vertical slice, not a horizontal layer.”
We didn’t build all of one persona’s features first. We built one thread through the whole product.
“It already delivers the vision.”
After Sprint 1, Wasl is minimally complete. Everything after is enhancement.
“It’s demonstrable at sprint review.”
Astrid checks in, Magnus sees it, Lars gets escalated if needed.
“Every story passes INVEST.”
No Larges made it in; all stories are independent and testable.
